🌍 Anatolia and the Middle East

Region

Also known as: Middle East, Near East, Anatolia, Asia Minor

One of the cradles of goat, sheep and cattle domestication; ayran, yogurt and camel milk among nomads.

The Middle East is one of the cradles of dairying. It was here, in the Fertile Crescent, that goats, sheep and cattle were domesticated some 10,000–11,000 years ago. Traces of milk fat on pottery from Anatolia show that the first farmers of the peninsula’s northwest were already processing milk in the 7th millennium BC.

The hot climate dictated the main way to preserve milk: souring it. Yogurt and ayran, a diluted salty drink, are staples of Turkish cuisine; strained sour milk becomes labneh, and whey and milk are turned into many cheeses, including Cypriot halloumi. For centuries, the Bedouin nomads of the deserts lived on camel milk, especially on long journeys.

The region combines settled farming with pastoral herding, so dairy here is both everyday food and a sign of hospitality.

Frequently asked questions

Where did people first start milking animals?

Archaeological evidence places some of the earliest signs of dairying in the Middle East and Anatolia.

What is ayran?

A fermented milk drink made of yogurt diluted with water, usually salted; popular in Turkey and neighboring countries.

Why is milk soured in the Middle East?

In a hot climate fresh milk spoils quickly, while soured milk keeps longer.
