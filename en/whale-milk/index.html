🥛 Whale milk

Type of milk

Whale milk, as thick as paste.

Whale milk is one of the fattiest in nature. According to SeaWorld, in baleen whales it can contain up to 40–50% fat; its consistency is compared to a thick paste.

A whale calf suckles right under water: the female’s teats are hidden in slit-like folds on her belly. The mother is thought to eject the milk herself so that it mixes less with the water. Thanks to the rich milk, calves grow very fast: according to SeaWorld, a nursing blue whale calf gains about 90 kg a day, and grey whales double their weight in about three months. Nursing in baleen whales lasts roughly 4–11 months.

Like true seals, many baleen whales barely eat while nursing: the females give birth in warm but food-poor waters and live off stored fat, feeding in cold, productive areas. Humans do not use whale milk, and direct measurements of its composition in large whales are rare, so many figures are generalised.

Frequently asked questions

How do baby whales drink milk underwater?

The mother’s teats are hidden in folds on her belly; the calf presses against them and drinks the thick milk right in the water.

How much fat is in whale milk?

According to various sources, about 30–50% in baleen whales — roughly ten times as much as in cow’s milk.
