🧑‍🌾 Milker

Job

Also known as: milking operator, milkmaid

A milking machine operator: attaches the unit and looks after udder hygiene.

A milker (a milkmaid or milkman) is a worker who milks cows. In the past, cows were milked by hand. Today almost all cows in developed countries are milked by machine. So the job is often called “milking machine operator.”

The work follows a set order. First the udder and teats are cleaned. Then the teat cups are put on. The machine switches between suction (a vacuum) and normal pressure, and the milk flows through tubes. Then it is filtered, cooled and collected in a large cooling tank. One machine milking takes 5–7 minutes on average. A cow is milked 2–3 times a day.

The key to the job is cleanliness. If the equipment is dirty, bacteria get into the udder, and the cow gets mastitis, an inflammation of the udder. Milk from a sick cow is of lower quality. The milker is one of the first to notice such problems and tells the vet. The job is very old. The English word “dairy” is linked to an old word that meant a female servant. After all, milking was usually done by women — dairymaids.

Frequently asked questions

What does a milker do?

Prepares the udder for milking, attaches the milking machine, keeps the udder clean and healthy and makes sure the milk is cooled right away.

How many times a day is a cow milked?

Usually 2–3 times a day. One machine milking takes 5–7 minutes on average.

Why is hygiene important when milking?

Dirty equipment lets bacteria into the udder, and the cow can get mastitis, an inflammation of the udder.
