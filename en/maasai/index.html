🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Maasai

People

Also known as: Masai, Maasai people

Herders of East Africa: milk is the staple food and cattle are the measure of wealth.

The Maasai are a herding people of southern Kenya and northern Tanzania. Their way of life is built around the herd: zebu cows, as well as goats and sheep, provide food and serve as a measure of wealth and the basis of exchange, bride price and gifts.

Traditionally, milk is the main food of the Maasai, especially for children and young warriors. It is drunk fresh and soured and kept in calabashes cleaned with smoke from smouldering twigs, which gives the milk a special taste. Meat is eaten at festivals and ceremonies; in the dry season milk is sometimes mixed with blood drawn from a live bull without slaughtering it.

Men’s age transitions are marked by the Enkipaata, Eunoto and Olng’esherr ceremonies — the last one with a festive meat feast. In 2018 UNESCO inscribed them on its list of heritage in need of urgent safeguarding. Droughts, schooling and farming are changing this way of life, and today many Maasai also eat maize, rice and drink tea with milk.

Frequently asked questions

What do the Maasai eat?

Traditionally milk, fresh and soured, and meat on special occasions; today also maize, rice, beans and tea with milk.

Why are cows so important to the Maasai?

The herd provides milk — the main food — and serves as a measure of wealth and the basis of exchange and bride price.

Do the Maasai drink blood mixed with milk?

Traditionally, sometimes — especially in the dry season and on special occasions; the blood is taken from a live animal.
