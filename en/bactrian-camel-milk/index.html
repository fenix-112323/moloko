🥛 Bactrian camel milk

Type of milk

Milk of the two-humped camel: richer than the dromedary's.

The Bactrian is the two-humped camel that lives in the cold deserts and steppes of Central Asia: in Mongolia, north-western China, Kazakhstan and neighbouring regions. According to an estimate cited by the FAO, of the world’s roughly 18 million camels only about 2 million are two-humped, so globally there is little Bactrian milk, although it matters to nomadic households.

In protein and lactose content Bactrian milk is close to that of the one-humped camel, but it usually has more fat — about 5% versus 3–4%. According to the FAO, its share of saturated fatty acids is lower than in cow’s milk. As with the dromedary, Bactrian milk contains no measurable β-lactoglobulin, and it has more protective proteins — lysozyme, lactoferrin, immunoglobulins — than cow’s milk.

Two-humped camel milk is drunk fresh but more often soured: in Kazakhstan and neighbouring countries it is made into shubat, and in Mongolia into khoormog. There is noticeably less data on the composition of Bactrian milk in the scientific literature than on dromedary milk, so the figures should be taken as approximate.

Frequently asked questions

How does Bactrian camel milk differ from dromedary milk?

They have roughly the same protein and lactose, but Bactrian milk is usually richer — about 5% fat versus 3–4% in the dromedary.

What is shubat?

A fermented camel milk drink, traditional in Kazakhstan and neighbouring parts of Central Asia.
