🧀 Baked milk

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Slow-cooked milk with a beige colour and a caramel taste.

Baked milk is milk that is brought to the boil and then heated slowly and evenly just below boiling point, usually for many hours. During this time some of the water evaporates and lactose undergoes the Maillard reaction with proteins: the milk turns creamy beige and gains a caramel aroma and a richer taste. A browned skin forms on the surface.

In traditional East Slavic cooking, milk was simmered in clay pots in a cooling masonry stove; today it is made by dairy plants, and at home it is prepared in an oven, slow cooker or thermos. Baked milk is the base for ryazhenka and varenets; it is drunk on its own and used in baking.

Baked milk differs from ordinary pasteurised milk in colour, taste and slightly higher fat content due to water evaporation. Some heat-sensitive vitamins are destroyed during long heating, but the product keeps longer than raw milk. Similar techniques exist in other cuisines: in South Asia milk is reduced to a thick mass for sweets (khoa, rabri).

Frequently asked questions

How is baked milk made?

Milk is brought to the boil and then simmered for several hours just below boiling until it turns beige. At home this can be done in an oven or slow cooker.

Why is baked milk beige?

During long heating, milk sugar reacts with proteins (the Maillard reaction), giving a creamy colour and a caramel taste.

What is made from baked milk?

Ryazhenka and varenets are made from baked milk; it is also drunk and used in baking.
