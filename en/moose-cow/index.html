🦌 Moose cow

Animal

Also known as: female moose, elk (Europe), Alces alces

Moose milk is a rarity: moose farms; it was used in therapeutic diets.

Moose milk is a great rarity. Moose are milked on a few experimental moose farms: the best known is the Sumarokovo farm in Kostroma Oblast, founded in the 1960s; a similar farm operates in the Pechora-Ilych Nature Reserve (Komi Republic). A small amount of moose cheese was also made on a farm in Sweden.

A moose cow is milked by hand, usually from the 4th–7th day after the calf is born, and comes to milking on her own, out of habit. Yield ranges from 1 to 6 litres a day, about 2.5 litres on average. The milk is very nutritious — more than 10% fat and more than 8% protein — and rich in lysozyme. It has been used in therapeutic diets, for example for certain stomach diseases, but such recommendations rest on limited data.

Moose are hard to domesticate and the farm herds are small, so moose milk will never become a mass-market product.

Frequently asked questions

Can you milk a moose?

Yes, on moose farms the cows are trained to be milked by hand; the milk is obtained in small amounts.

How much milk does a moose give?

From 1 to 6 litres a day, about 2.5 litres on average.

How much fat is in moose milk?

More than 10% fat and more than 8% protein — much richer than cow’s milk.
