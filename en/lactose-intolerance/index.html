❤️ Lactose intolerance

Health

Also known as: lactase deficiency

Without the enzyme lactase, milk causes discomfort. Estimated to affect most adults worldwide (from <30% in northern Europe to >70% in parts of Asia and Africa).

Lactose intolerance is when dairy causes bloating, wind, tummy pain or diarrhoea. The cause is that the body makes too little lactase. Lactase is an enzyme that breaks down lactose, the sugar in milk. Undigested lactose reaches the large intestine, where bacteria ferment it.

It is very common. According to the NIDDK (US), about 68% of people in the world digest lactose poorly. It is most common in Africa and Asia and least common in northern Europe. The reason is that in most mammals, lactase almost disappears once they stop feeding on milk. In some people, it stays for life. This is linked to the long history of dairy farming. Do not confuse intolerance with milk allergy. An allergy is an immune reaction to the protein, and it can be dangerous.

Many people with intolerance can have a little lactose without problems. Small portions, fermented milk products, hard cheeses and lactose-free milk usually help. Lactase tablets can be taken before meals. The NHS advises not to cut out dairy completely without advice from a doctor or dietitian, because you may not get enough calcium.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I'm lactose intolerant?

Usually minutes or hours after milk you get bloating, wind, tummy pain or diarrhoea. A doctor can find the exact cause.

Can I drink kefir if I'm lactose intolerant?

Often yes. Kefir and yoghurt have less lactose because bacteria have eaten some of it. But tolerance varies from person to person.

How many people are lactose intolerant?

According to the NIDDK, about 68% of people in the world digest lactose poorly. It is least common in northern Europe.
