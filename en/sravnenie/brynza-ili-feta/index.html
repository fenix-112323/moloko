Brynza vs feta: what's the difference

Both are white brined cheeses, salty and rindless. But "brynza" is a general name for cheese made from various kinds of milk, while "feta" is a protected name for a Greek cheese with strict rules.

🧀 Bryndza🧀 Feta
Raw materialCow's, goat's, sheep's or buffalo milk, or a mixSheep's milk or a mix with goat's milk (no more than 30% goat's)
How it's madeA brined cheese: ripens in 18–22% brine for several days or weeks (depending on the method)The salted curd is kept in brine (about 7% salt) for several weeks, then for at least 2 months in the cold
Taste and textureTangy, moderately salty; firm, often sturdy and slightly crumblyPiquant and salty, from mild to sharp; soft, crumbly, slightly grainy
Fat contentDepends on the type of milk and the recipeAt least 43% fat in dry matter; about 21 g of fat per 100 g
OriginThe Carpathians; common in the Balkans, Moldova, the Caucasus and Eastern European cuisinesMainland Greece and the island of Lesbos
Name statusA general nameA Protected Designation of Origin in the EU since 2002

Which to choose

For Greek salad and Mediterranean-style baking, use feta: it has a mild, creamy character. Brynza is usually firmer and can be saltier; you can soften its saltiness by soaking the cheese in milk or water.
