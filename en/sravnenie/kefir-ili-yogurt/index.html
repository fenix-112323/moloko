Kefir vs yogurt: what's the difference

Both are made by fermenting milk, but with different starters. Kefir is a drinkable product made with kefir grains, in which both bacteria and yeasts are at work. Classic yogurt is fermented with just two species of lactic acid bacteria.

🥛 Kefir🥣 Yogurt
StarterKefir grains: lactic streptococci, lactobacilli, acetic acid bacteria and yeasts — around twenty species of microorganismsLactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus
How it's madeLactic and alcoholic fermentation of cow's milk; the grains are strained out and reusedMilk is pasteurized (75–95 °C) and inoculated; the bacteria turn lactose into lactic acid, then the product is cooled
AlcoholTrace amounts: about 0.07% in one-day kefir and up to 0.88% in three-day kefirNo alcoholic fermentation
Taste and textureA white, runny drink, sometimes slightly fizzySmooth and moderately thick; proteins form a network that holds water and fat
Fat contentFrom under 0.5% to about 9%; classic kefir is 2.5%Depends on the milk it is made from
OriginNorth Caucasus; the Elbrus area is considered the home of the grains, and the starter is linked to the Karachays and BalkarsBulgaria is generally considered its homeland; the bacterium was named bulgaricus in 1907

Which to choose

If you want a refreshing drink with a light tang and a "live", complex starter, choose kefir. For a spoonable breakfast, sauces and desserts, thick yogurt is more convenient. For children and anyone avoiding even traces of alcohol, yogurt is the more sensible choice.
