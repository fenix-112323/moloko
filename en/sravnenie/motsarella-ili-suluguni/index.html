Mozzarella vs sulguni: what's the difference

Both are pasta filata ("stretched-curd") cheeses: the curd is kneaded in hot water until it becomes elastic. But mozzarella is a delicate fresh cheese from Italy, while sulguni is a firmer, saltier brined cheese from Georgia.

🧀 Mozzarella🧀 Sulguni
Raw materialClassically, milk from black water buffalo (mozzarella di bufala campana); more often cow's milk (fior di latte)Pasteurized cow's or buffalo milk, or a mix of the two
How it's madeThe curd is heated and kneaded in hot water, cut into portions (mozzatura) and dropped into cold brineThe curd is cheddared, then kneaded in water or whey at 70–80 °C until stretchy
Brining and ripeningA short time in brine; ripened for 1–3 days17–18% whey-based brine at 8–12 °C for 6 hours to 1–2 days
Taste and textureDelicate, soft, slightly bland; a thin, glossy skin, releases whey when cutClean, tangy, moderately salty; firm, layered, elastic, with no rind
OriginCampania, ItalyGeorgia; Mingrelia or Svaneti is named as its homeland

Which to choose

For caprese salad and cold starters where delicacy matters, choose mozzarella. For khachapuri, baking, and anyone who likes a saltier taste and a firm, layered texture, sulguni is the better fit.
