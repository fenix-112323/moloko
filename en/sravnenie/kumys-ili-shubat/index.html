Kumis vs shubat: what's the difference

These are two nomadic fermented milk drinks of Central Asia. Kumis is made from mare's milk and shubat from camel's milk; they differ in fat content, texture and how long they keep.

🍶 Kumis🍶 Shubat
Raw materialMare's milkCamel's milk
How it's madeLactic and alcoholic fermentation by Lactobacillus bulgaricus, Lactobacillus acidophilus and yeastsFresh milk with a starter is left for about a day in a wooden tub; it isn't shaken but is stirred well before serving
AlcoholUsually 0.5–2.5%, strong kumis up to 4.5%Not stated in the source
Taste and textureFrothy, fizzy, sweet-and-sourThicker than kumis, white
Fat contentLower than shubatUp to 8%
Shelf lifeA fresh fermented productTurns sour within a day; keeps longer only at no more than +5 °C, so it is hardly ever exported
OriginKazakhs, Kyrgyz, Mongols, Bashkirs, Tatars and other steppe peoples; Herodotus wrote about mare's milk among the ScythiansKazakhs and Uzbeks; in Turkmenistan a similar drink is called chal

Which to choose

Kumis is for those who want a light, frothy, refreshing drink. Shubat is more filling and thicker; it's best drunk where it's made, as it sours quickly.
