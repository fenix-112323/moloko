Butter vs ghee: what's the difference

Ghee is made from butter: it is simmered until the water evaporates and the milk solids settle and brown. The result is almost pure milk fat with a nutty aroma.

🧈 Butter🧈 Ghee
Raw materialCreamButter
How it's madeCream is churned: the fat globules clump together and the buttermilk separatesButter is simmered for a long time, the foam is skimmed off and the clear fat is poured off the sediment; longer than ordinary clarified butter
Fat contentUsually 72.5–82.5%About 99%
TasteCreamyNutty and aromatic; heated to 120 °C, the flavour is stronger than at 100 °C
For fryingBurns over high heat because of its proteins and waterSmoke point around 250 °C, suitable for frying
Shelf lifeIn the fridgeLonger than butter; kept in a dry, airtight glass jar away from sunlight
OriginKnown in the Middle East since the Neolithic; a Sumerian tablet from around 2500 BC describes how it was madeThe Indian subcontinent; used in cooking and rituals

Which to choose

For sandwiches, dough and sauces over low heat, use butter. For high-heat frying, Indian dishes and long storage, ghee is more convenient. Note that ghee is still a dairy product.
