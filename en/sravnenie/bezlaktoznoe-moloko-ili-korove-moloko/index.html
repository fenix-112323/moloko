Lactose-free vs regular milk: what's the difference

Lactose-free milk is ordinary cow's milk in which the milk sugar, lactose, has been broken down or removed. It is intended for people with lactose intolerance.

🌱 Lactose-free milk🥛 Cow's milk
How it's madeThe enzyme lactase is added to the milk to break lactose down into glucose and galactose; ultrafiltration is also usedNo enzyme treatment
SugarGlucose and galactose instead of lactoseLactose
TasteTastes sweeter with the same amount of sugar; slightly less viscousFamiliar taste
Who it's forPeople with lactose intolerance; not suitable for a milk protein allergyPeople who digest lactose normally
HistoryThe lactose-reduction process was developed in the early 1980s (Virginia Holsinger); the first fully lactose-free milk for consumers was launched by Finland's Valio in 2001—

Which to choose

If milk gives you discomfort because of lactose, lactose-free milk lets you keep dairy in your diet. If you have no problems, there's no particular reason to switch; and it won't help with a protein allergy.
