Condensed milk vs powdered milk: what's the difference

Both are preserved dairy products, invented so that milk would keep for a long time. Condensed milk is cooked down under vacuum with sugar, while powdered milk is dried almost completely.

🥫 Condensed milk🧀 Milk powder
How it's madeMilk is pasteurized at 85–87 °C, condensed in a vacuum pan at 55–60 °C, and sugar syrup is addedPasteurized milk is concentrated to about 50% solids and sprayed into a hot chamber; less often it is roller-dried or freeze-dried
CompositionNo more than 26.5% moisture, about 43.5% sucrose, about 8.5% fat (sweetened whole)Almost no water
Taste and textureThick, very sweetA powder; roller drying can give it a "cooked" taste
Shelf lifeKeeps for a long time without significant changes in its propertiesKeeps much longer than liquid milk without a fridge; spoiled by moisture and heat
UsesIn tea and coffee; boiled condensed milk for creams, baking and dessertsReconstituted milk, baking, confectionery, infant formula, food aid, hiking
HistoryNicolas Appert condensed milk in France in 1820; Gail Borden's patent dates from 1856Marco Polo described sun-dried skimmed milk among Mongol warriors; T. S. Grimwade's patent dates from 1855

Which to choose

For desserts, coffee and as a treat in its own right, choose condensed milk. If you need a store of "regular" milk, an ingredient for dough or something light for a hike, choose powdered milk.
