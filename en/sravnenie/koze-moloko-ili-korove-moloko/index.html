Goat milk vs cow milk: what's the difference

Goat and cow milk are similar in composition but differ in fat globule size, protein makeup and taste. Goat milk is sometimes called "hypoallergenic", but the sources do not support this.

🥛 Goat's milk🥛 Cow's milk
Fat globulesSmaller: roughly 0.1–2 µm; the cream stays suspended longer, so homogenization isn't neededLarger: roughly 2–4 µm; the cream rises faster
Fat contentAccording to ru.wikipedia, 4.2–4.4%; according to en.wikipedia, about 3.5 g per 100 g (depends on breed and feed)About 3.2–3.9%
ProteinsCasein makes up about 75% of the protein; no αs1- or γ-caseinCasein makes up about 80% of the protein
LactoseSlightly less, but close to cow milk levels, so it can still cause problems for people with lactose intoleranceAbout 4.4–4.5% carbohydrates
AllergyChildren allergic to cow milk protein often can't have goat milk either: the likelihood of an allergy is highOne of the most common food allergens in children
Taste and colourA distinctive smell and taste that not everyone likes; goat milk butter is whiteFamiliar mild taste; the butter is yellowish because of beta-carotene
For infantsNot recommended on its own (low in iron, folate, and vitamins C and D); goat milk-based formulas existAlso not recommended on its own for babies under one year

Which to choose

Goat milk is a good choice for cheeses (such as brynza and pélardon) and for people who enjoy its taste. Don't pick it as a substitute for cow milk in a milk protein allergy without a doctor's advice, and it does little to help with lactose intolerance.
