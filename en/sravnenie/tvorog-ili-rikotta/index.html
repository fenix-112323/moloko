Tvorog vs ricotta: what's the difference

They look alike but are made from different raw materials. Tvorog (farmer's cheese) is made by fermenting milk, while ricotta is a "recooked" cheese made from whey left over from other cheeses.

🥣 Tvorog🧀 Ricotta
Raw materialMilk (often skimmed, then mixed with cream)Whey from mozzarella or other cheeses; sometimes a little milk is added
Main proteinCaseinWhey proteins (albumin, lactalbumin)
How it's madeMilk is fermented; the protein is coagulated by lactic acid (acid method) or with rennet as well (acid-rennet method); the whey is pressed outWhey is heated to about 80–90 °C, the curds float up and are collected and drained
Taste and textureFirm texture, tangy tasteDelicate, slightly sweet thanks to lactose
Fat contentFrom fat-free to full-fat (about 19–23%)From about 8% (from cow whey) to 24% (from sheep whey)
OriginTraditional in Eastern, Northern and parts of Central EuropeSouthern Italy: Sicily, Campania, Apulia, Calabria
Shelf lifeHighly perishable, keep refrigerated onlyFresh: up to 10 days in the fridge; baked ricotta: up to 2 months

Which to choose

For syrniki (cheese pancakes), bakes and a tangy breakfast, tvorog is the better fit. For Italian baking, lasagne and delicate fillings, choose ricotta with its slightly sweet taste.
