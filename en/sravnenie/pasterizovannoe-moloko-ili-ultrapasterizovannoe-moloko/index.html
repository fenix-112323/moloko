Pasteurized vs UHT milk: what's the difference

Both methods heat milk to kill pathogens, but at different temperatures and for different times. The method determines the shelf life and whether the milk needs refrigerating before the pack is opened.

🥛 Pasteurized milk🥛 UHT milk
Heat treatmentHigh-temperature short-time pasteurization (HTST): 71.5 °C for 15 seconds, or 63 °C for 30 minutesAbout 135 °C for 1–2 seconds
Shelf lifeAbout two weeks in the fridgeAbout three months unrefrigerated in special aseptic packaging
PackagingOrdinary containers; must be kept coldAseptic packaging (such as multilayer cartons); aseptic Tetra Pak cartons appeared in 1961
SafetyPasteurization effectively kills pathogens from raw milk without significantly affecting nutritional value (FDA)Almost sterile until the pack is opened
HistoryPasteur proposed brief heating for wine in 1864; Franz von Soxhlet proposed it for milk in 1886Industrial plants from the 1950s–1960s; UHT milk became widely available in the 1970s
PopularityCommon wherever there is a well-developed cold chainIn Europe, about seven in ten people drink it regularly; less popular in northern Europe and Scandinavia

Which to choose

If you'll finish the milk in a few days and have a fridge to hand, pasteurized milk will do. For stocking up, travel and places without reliable refrigeration, UHT milk is more convenient. Once opened, both kinds are kept in the fridge.
