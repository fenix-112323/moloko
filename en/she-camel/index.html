🐪 She-camel

Animal

Also known as: camel, dromedary, naqa, Camelus dromedarius

Deserts of the Middle East, Africa and Kazakhstan. Its milk is made into shubat.

Camels are milked in the deserts and semi-deserts of Africa and Asia, mostly by nomadic herders. According to the FAO, camel milk makes up only about 0.4% of world milk output, but in dry areas it can be the main source: in northern Kenya camels give far more milk than cows. The largest producers are Kenya, Somalia and Pakistan.

About 5 liters a day is considered a good yield; in Africa a lactation usually gives 1,000–2,700 liters. Yield peaks in the second or third month, and lactation lasts 8 to 18 months. The one-humped dromedary usually gives more milk than the two-humped Bactrian camel.

Camel milk is close to cow’s milk in fat, but it contains no beta-lactoglobulin and more vitamin C. It is drunk fresh or fermented: Central Asia makes shubat (chal among the Turkmen), East Africa suusac, and Sudan gariss.

Frequently asked questions

How much milk does a camel give per day?

On pasture usually about 5 liters; in the rainy season the yield is roughly double that of the dry season.

What is made from camel milk?

The fermented drinks shubat and chal, suusac and gariss; commercially, pasteurized milk, chocolate and soft cheeses.

How long does a camel give milk?

From 8 to 18 months after the calf is born.
