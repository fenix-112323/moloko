🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Zulu

People

Also known as: amaZulu, Zulu people

Amasi is considered a food that gives strength and health; customs and taboos surround it.

For the Zulu, the largest people of South Africa, cattle have historically been a measure of wealth and part of ceremonies, and milk an important food. Fresh milk was rarely drunk: it was soured into amasi.

Raw cow’s milk is left to ferment for one to five days in a calabash or leather bag; the thin whey (umlaza) is poured off and the thick remainder is eaten, usually with maize porridge. In Zulu tradition amasi is considered a food that gives strength and health. It also carries taboos: people in a ritually “impure” state — for example, after contact with death — are by custom expected to abstain from amasi.

Today amasi is also made industrially: the milk is pasteurised and fermented with starter cultures containing Lactococcus lactis. It is sold throughout Southern Africa under the name “maas”. Researchers note that the composition of homemade amasi is still poorly studied.

Frequently asked questions

What is amasi?

A thick sour milk from Southern Africa: raw cow’s milk ferments for several days and the whey is poured off.

What is amasi eaten with?

Most often with maize porridge, but it is also drunk.

What is umlaza?

The thin whey poured off when making amasi.
