🦙 Llama

Animal

Also known as: Lama glama

A pack animal of the Andes.

The llama is a pack animal of the Andes, domesticated several thousand years ago. It is raised for carrying loads, wool and meat; llamas are hardly used for dairy, and the milk usually goes to the young.

Llama milk has been little studied. Available data suggest it has more lactose and less fat than cow’s milk, although results from different studies vary noticeably. There are reports of a salty, long-keeping cheese made from llama milk in the Andes, but there is little technical information about it.

Knowing the composition of llama milk matters mainly to veterinarians: to raise an orphaned baby, they choose a substitute close in composition to the mother’s milk. Goat’s milk or milk replacers for calves and lambs are usually used, but they do not always suit the young of South American camelids.

Frequently asked questions

Do people milk llamas?

Hardly ever: llamas are kept for carrying loads, wool and meat, and the milk is drunk by the young.

What is llama milk like?

According to the limited data, it has more lactose and less fat than cow’s milk.

What should you feed an orphaned baby llama?

Special milk replacers, on a veterinarian’s advice.
