❤️ Growth

Health

Milk is the food that makes the young grow.

Milk is the first food of every young mammal. Its main job is to help the baby grow fast. Milk has everything needed for this at once: protein for muscles and organs, fat and lactose for energy, calcium and phosphorus for bones, and vitamins.

In different animals, milk is tuned to how fast the young grow. A calf grows quickly, so cow's milk has a lot of protein, especially casein. A human baby grows more slowly, and human milk has noticeably less protein. But it has more sugars that feed helpful gut bacteria. That is why cow's milk is not suitable for babies instead of breast milk. The NHS advises not giving cow's milk as a main drink before age one.

After age one, milk can be a handy part of a child's diet. The NHS says that from one year a child can have pasteurized whole cow's milk as a drink. Until age two, full-fat dairy products are better. Children aged 1–3 need about 350 mg of calcium a day — that is about 300 ml of milk. Other foods, sleep and exercise matter just as much for growth.

Frequently asked questions

Does milk help children grow?

Milk gives protein, calcium and energy that growth needs. But growth depends on the whole diet, sleep, health and genes.

At what age can a child drink cow's milk?

The NHS advises giving cow's milk as a main drink only from age one. Before that, breast milk or infant formula.
