🏺 Traditional dairy products

Concept

Also known as: folk dairy products, ethnic dairy products

Products made for centuries to folk recipes: fermenting, cheesemaking, slow baking, drying. Tied to regions and peoples.

Traditional dairy products are foods that have been made for centuries from folk recipes, long before refrigerators and dairy plants. Their main purpose is to preserve perishable milk. Several basic methods were used for this: fermentation (kefir, matsoni, katyk, kumis, shubat, dahi), cheesemaking (from brynza and feta to Parmigiano Reggiano), separating and rendering fat (butter, ghee), drying (kurt, chhurpi) and slow baking (baked milk, ryazhenka).

These products are closely tied to regions, peoples and animal species: mare’s milk among steppe nomads, camel milk in deserts, yak milk in the Himalayas, buffalo milk in South Asia and Italy. Starters are often passed on “from batch to batch”: a spoonful of the previous batch is added to fresh milk, so every household develops its own microflora. According to the FAO, many such products are still made in households and small dairies in Africa, Asia and Latin America.

Traditional products differ from technologically developed ones in their origin and method of production, although the line is blurry: kefir, yogurt and feta are now made industrially, and in many cases their names are protected by law as part of cultural heritage.

Frequently asked questions

Which dairy products are considered traditional?

Those made for centuries from folk recipes: kefir, kumis, matsoni, ayran, tvorog, cheeses, butter, ghee, kurt and others.

Why do different cultures have different dairy products?

They depend on the climate, the animal species (cow, mare, camel, yak) and the need to preserve milk — by fermenting, drying or rendering the fat.
