🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Lebanese

People

Also known as: Lebanese people

Labneh is a thick strained yogurt; labneh balls are kept in olive oil.

Lebanese cuisine is rich in dairy products, many of which have for centuries helped preserve milk from spring to winter. Milk comes from goats, sheep and cows; the mountain areas are especially known for goat’s milk.

The most popular product is labneh, a thick strained yogurt. It is eaten for breakfast with olive oil and flatbread, and labneh anbaris is aged in clay jars. Labneh balls are stored in olive oil. Shanklish is an aged cheese with a spiced coating, often of dried thyme; kishk is a dried mixture of yogurt and bulgur used to make soup in winter. Akkawi and halloumi cheeses and ashta, a cream used in desserts, are also common.

Today Lebanon meets a large part of its milk demand through imports, and researchers note that traditional long-keeping products can support the food resilience of local farms.

Frequently asked questions

What is shanklish?

A Lebanese aged cheese shaped into balls, usually rolled in dried thyme and spices.

What is kishk?

A dried mixture of yogurt and bulgur used to make a thick soup, especially in winter.

Why are labneh balls stored in oil?

The oil seals the product off from the air and helps it keep for a long time.
