🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Kazakhs

People

Also known as: Kazakh people, Qazaqs

A nomadic herding culture: kumis, shubat, kurt.

For centuries the Kazakhs practised nomadic and semi-nomadic herding in the steppes and semi-deserts, moving herds of horses, sheep, goats, cows and camels between summer and winter pastures. Dairy food was central to this way of life, especially in summer, when the animals give the most milk.

The main drink is koumiss (qymyz) made from mare’s milk; camel’s milk is made into shubat. Kurt — salty balls that keep for months — is pressed and dried from soured milk. Ayran, cream and clarified butter are also common. Offering a guest koumiss is a sign of respect and hospitality.

The horse breeder’s calendar is tied to koumiss. The spring rites of Kazakh horse breeders — “bie bailau” (the first milking of the mares) and “qymyz muryndyq” (the opening of the koumiss season) — were inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2018. Today most Kazakhs live a settled life, but koumiss and shubat remain popular and are also produced industrially.

Frequently asked questions

Why do Kazakhs drink koumiss?

Koumiss made from mare’s milk is the traditional drink of steppe horse breeders: it quenches thirst well, is nourishing and is linked to customs of hospitality.

What is qymyz muryndyq?

A spring rite that opens the koumiss season, when people are treated to the first koumiss; part of the spring rites of Kazakh horse breeders inscribed by UNESCO.

What is shubat?

A fermented camel milk drink popular in Kazakhstan.
