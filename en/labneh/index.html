🫙 Labneh

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: labne, labaneh, strained yogurt

A thick, salty strained yogurt of the Levant, denser than Greek yogurt. In Lebanon and Palestine it is rolled into balls, coated in za'atar and kept in olive oil.

Labneh is a thick, slightly salty strained yogurt from the Levant (Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Jordan and neighbouring countries). Yogurt made from cow’s, goat’s or sheep’s milk is mixed with salt and hung in a cloth bag for several hours or overnight until most of the whey has drained off. The result is a soft mass, denser than Greek yogurt, with a fresh, tangy taste.

Labneh is eaten for breakfast and as part of meze: spread on a plate, drizzled with olive oil, sprinkled with za’atar or mint and eaten with pita, olives and vegetables. Strained longer, labneh becomes firm enough to roll into balls; these are dried a little, coated in za’atar or pepper and kept in olive oil, which preserves them for months.

Labneh differs from Greek yogurt in being denser and saltier, and from cream cheese in being a strained cultured milk product with no cream. Its relatives are South Asian chakka (the base of shrikhand), Icelandic skyr and Central Asian suzma.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between labneh and Greek yogurt?

Both are strained yogurt, but labneh is strained longer and salted, so it is thicker and closer to a soft cheese.

How do you make labneh at home?

Mix yogurt with salt, put it in cheesecloth and hang it overnight in the fridge so the whey drains off.

Why are labneh balls kept in oil?

Olive oil keeps the firm balls away from air and helps them keep longer.
