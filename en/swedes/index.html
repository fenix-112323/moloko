🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Swedes

People

Filmjölk (fil) is a soured milk that may have been made at home as early as the Viking Age.

Milk and fermented milk products have a firm place in Swedish cuisine: they are eaten for breakfast and added to baking and sauces. The milk comes mainly from cows; in the past, in the north of the country, cattle were driven to distant forest pastures in summer.

The best-known Swedish product is filmjölk, or simply fil: a mild sour milk fermented at room temperature by the mesophilic bacteria Lactococcus lactis and Leuconostoc mesenteroides. It is eaten with a spoon with muesli, cereal, crushed crispbread, jam or berries. Similar products are described in written sources from the 18th century; the idea that homemade fil was made as far back as the Viking Age is only a guess. Filbunke, fil that has set right in the bowl, is also well known.

Today filmjölk is a mass-market product: it went on sale in 1931, and later versions with different fat levels and flavourings appeared. Homemade starters are still kept and passed down in families.

Frequently asked questions

What is filmjölk?

A mild Swedish sour milk fermented by mesophilic bacteria at room temperature and eaten with a spoon, often with muesli or berries.

What is the difference between fil and yogurt?

Yogurt is fermented by thermophilic bacteria at 40–45 °C, while fil is fermented by mesophilic bacteria at room temperature, so it is milder and less sour.

What do Swedes eat fil with?

Usually for breakfast — with muesli, cereal, crushed crispbread, jam, fruit or cinnamon.
