🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Sámi

People

Also known as: Saami, Sámi people

Reindeer herders of northern Europe; reindeer milk and cheese in traditional cooking.

The Sámi are the Indigenous people of northern Scandinavia, Finland and the Kola Peninsula, historically reindeer herders, fishers and hunters. For them the reindeer is transport, food and material for clothing.

Until the 20th century the Sámi milked reindeer does. Reindeer milk is very rich (10–20% fat), and even a small yield was valuable. It was drunk, added to coffee, thickened and made into cheese, which was dried and stored for winter. Reindeer milk was also frozen in small casks for use in winter.

From the 20th century reindeer herding became larger in scale and more mobile, herds were kept in enclosures less often, and milking almost disappeared; diseases that spread when animals were crowded together also played a part. Today reindeer milk and cheese are rare: only a few farms make them, partly to keep the tradition alive.

Frequently asked questions

Did the Sámi milk reindeer?

Yes, until the 20th century milking reindeer does was part of the Sámi economy; today it is rare.

What was reindeer milk used for?

It was drunk, added to coffee, thickened, made into cheese and frozen for winter.

Why did the Sámi stop milking reindeer?

Reindeer herding became larger and more mobile, and diseases spread when animals were kept crowded together.
