🌍 Urals and Volga region

Region

Also known as: Volga-Ural region, Idel-Ural

A region of Turkic and Finno-Ugric peoples: kumis, katyk, kurt.

The Urals and the Middle Volga are a transition zone between forest and steppe, where Turkic peoples (Tatars, Bashkirs, Chuvash) and Finno-Ugric peoples (Mari, Udmurts, Mordvins) have long lived side by side. Steppe horse-breeding traditions and settled farming with cows gave the region a mixed dairy culture.

Bashkirs and Kazakhs of the steppe areas have long made kumis from mare’s milk; in the 19th–20th centuries kumis sanatoriums opened in the Southern Urals, where people came for kumis cures, although its therapeutic benefit has been only partly confirmed by science. Tatars and Bashkirs make katyk, a sour milk from baked or boiled milk, and from it suzma and kort (kurt), dried balls.

Among Finno-Ugric peoples, soured milk, tvorog (curd cheese) and smetana (sour cream) are common in baking and soups. Dairy dishes here are part of both everyday and festive cooking.

Frequently asked questions

What is katyk?

A fermented milk product made from boiled or baked milk, traditional among Tatars, Bashkirs and other Turkic peoples.

What is a kumis cure?

A tradition of drinking kumis at special sanatoriums; its benefits are being studied, but the scientific evidence is still limited.

What peoples live in the Urals and the Volga region?

Turkic peoples — Tatars, Bashkirs, Chuvash — and Finno-Ugric peoples — Mari, Udmurts, Mordvins — as well as many others.
