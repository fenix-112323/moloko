🥛 UHT milk

Type of milk

Also known as: ultra-high temperature milk, long-life milk

Heated to 135–150 °C for a few seconds and packed aseptically: months without a fridge.

UHT milk (ultra-high temperature) is milk that is heated very strongly and very briefly in a continuous flow and then filled into sterile packaging with no access for microbes (aseptic filling). As defined by the FAO and WHO, UHT treatment is usually carried out at 135–150 °C with a holding time sufficient for “commercial sterility”; a typical duration is 2 to 10 seconds.

Together with aseptic multilayer packaging, this allows unopened milk to be kept for months without refrigeration. Once opened, it must be kept cold like ordinary milk and drunk within a few days. Heating can be direct (steam is mixed with the milk) or indirect (through the wall of a heat exchanger).

According to the FAO, losses of most nutrients during UHT treatment are small, although whey protein denaturation and flavour changes are more pronounced than with pasteurisation: the milk acquires a slight “cooked” taste. Originally UHT milk was almost exclusively cow’s milk, but according to the FAO, UHT goat and buffalo milk is now sold in a number of countries.

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn’t UHT milk go off for months?

It is sterilised by brief heating to 135–150 °C and filled into sterile sealed packaging that microbes cannot enter.

Does UHT milk lose nutrients?

According to the FAO, the losses are small; certain vitamins and whey proteins are affected most, while protein, fat and calcium are retained.

What is the difference between UHT and sterilised milk?

UHT milk is heated in a continuous flow for a few seconds and filled aseptically, while sterilised milk is usually heated longer and already in its container, so it has a more “boiled” taste.
