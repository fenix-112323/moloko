🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Cantonese

People

Also known as: Cantonese people

The people of Guangdong, home of the "double skin milk" dessert made from buffalo milk.

The Cantonese are the people of Guangdong province in southern China. Milk was historically not a staple of their cuisine, but some areas of the Pearl River Delta developed their own dairy tradition based on buffalo.

Its best-known symbol is double-skin milk from Shunde district. Buffalo milk is boiled and cooled so that a skin forms on top, then mixed with egg white and sugar and steamed, which creates a second skin. The result is a delicate milk pudding eaten warm or cold, sometimes with red beans or fruit. Buffalo milk is valued for its high fat content, although the local swamp buffalo give little of it. Legends about the dessert’s origin date it to the late Qing dynasty.

The skills of making double-skin milk are on the intangible heritage lists of Shunde and, according to available information, since 2018 of Guangdong province. Today the dessert is served in dessert shops throughout Hong Kong, Macau and beyond China.

Frequently asked questions

What is double-skin milk?

A Cantonese milk pudding from Shunde made of buffalo milk, egg white and sugar; one skin forms on the cooled milk and the second on the finished pudding.

Why is buffalo milk used for double-skin milk?

It is richer than cow’s milk, so the pudding comes out smooth and dense.

Where does double-skin milk come from?

Shunde district in Guangdong province, southern China.
