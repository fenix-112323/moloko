🧀 Ayib

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: ayibe, Ethiopian cottage cheese

A soft Ethiopian curd cheese: soured milk (ergo) is churned into butter, and the remaining buttermilk is heated to 40–50 °C until the curds separate.

Ayib is a soft Ethiopian curd cheese. It is made from sour milk (ergo) or, more often, from the buttermilk left after churning butter from sour milk. The buttermilk is heated in a clay pot over a low fire to about 40–50 °C until the curd separates; the pot is then cooled, the whey is drained through a straw or fibre “filter” (for example, made from the fibres of enset, the “false banana”), and the curd is moved to a clean container.

According to the FAO, 8 litres of buttermilk yield about 1 kg of ayib; it contains roughly 80% water, about 15% protein and less than 2% fat. Because of its high moisture, ayib does not keep long — about a week; pasteurising the curd and removing more of the whey extend its shelf life.

Ayib is a crumbly, low-fat product, similar in texture to crumbly cottage cheese or ricotta. It is served with doro wat, a spicy chicken stew, and injera flatbread, mixed with stewed cabbage, butter and hot pepper, and eaten with kitfo (minced raw beef). Fresh ayib softens the heat of Ethiopian spices.

Frequently asked questions

What is ayib made from?

From buttermilk or sour milk, which is heated until the curd separates, and then the whey is drained off.

What do you eat ayib with?

With spicy dishes — doro wat, kitfo — and injera flatbread; it softens the heat of the spices.

How long does ayib keep?

Because of its high moisture, about a week.
