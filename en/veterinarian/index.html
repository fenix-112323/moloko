🧑‍⚕️ Veterinarian

Job

Also known as: vet

Treats animals and checks herd health and milk safety.

A veterinarian is a doctor for animals. On a dairy farm, the vet treats sick cows, gives vaccinations and looks after the health of the whole herd. The vet advises the farmer on how best to feed and keep the animals. Vets protect people too: they make sure diseases do not pass from animals to people and that food is safe.

A common disease in dairy cows is mastitis, an inflammation of the udder. It is caused by bacteria. A sign of the disease is a high number of somatic cells in the milk. These are mostly white blood cells that fight infection. A healthy cow usually has fewer than 100,000 of them per millilitre. In the European Union, pooled milk may have no more than 400,000 cells per millilitre.

If a cow is treated with medicines, her milk cannot be sent to the dairy plant for some time. The vet sets this period. The vet watches over calvings and makes sure the calf gets colostrum in time — the mother's first milk, rich in antibodies. There are also vet inspectors. They check animals and animal products for dangerous diseases and make sure food safety rules are followed.

Frequently asked questions

What does a vet do on a dairy farm?

Treats and vaccinates cows, looks after the health of the herd, helps with calvings and checks that the milk is safe.

What is mastitis in cows?

It is an inflammation of the udder, usually caused by bacteria. Milk from such a cow has many somatic cells, and its quality is lower.

Can you sell milk from a cow that is being treated?

Usually not: for some time after treatment the milk is not accepted. The vet decides how long.
