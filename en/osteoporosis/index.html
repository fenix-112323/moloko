❤️ Osteoporosis

Health

Brittle bones, linked to a lack of calcium and vitamin D.

Osteoporosis is a disease that makes bones fragile so they break easily. It develops slowly, over years, and is often noticed only after a fracture. The wrist, hip and vertebrae break most often. With severe osteoporosis, a bone can crack even from a cough or a sneeze.

According to the NHS, osteoporosis is more common in women, especially after menopause. But men get it too. The risk goes up with family history, low body weight, smoking, heavy drinking, an inactive life and long-term use of certain medicines. Strong bones need calcium and vitamin D. Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium.

Milk and dairy products are a handy source of calcium. That is why they are often linked with preventing osteoporosis. To protect your bones, the NHS advises regular exercise, eating foods with calcium, getting vitamin D (for many people, a 10 mcg daily supplement), stopping smoking and drinking less alcohol. Milk alone will not protect you from osteoporosis. Your whole way of life matters. If you are at risk, a doctor can check, for example with a bone density scan.

Frequently asked questions

Does milk help prevent osteoporosis?

Milk gives calcium that bones need. But exercise, vitamin D, not smoking and drinking in moderation also matter for prevention.

Who is most likely to get osteoporosis?

According to the NHS, women after menopause most often, but men get it too. Smoking, alcohol, low body weight and family history raise the risk.
