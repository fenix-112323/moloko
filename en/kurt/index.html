🧀 Kurt

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: qurt, qurut

Dried balls of fermented, strained milk: nomads' food that keeps for months.

Kurt is a dried fermented milk product of the peoples of Central Asia: hard, salty balls or patties of strained sour milk. Soured milk, katyk or buttermilk is boiled or simply strained through cloth to remove the whey; the thick mass is salted, rolled into balls and dried in the sun and wind, sometimes lightly smoked. Dried kurt keeps for months.

Nomadic and semi-nomadic peoples have long used this way of preserving milk protein for winter and for travel: Kazakhs, Kyrgyz, Turkmen, Uzbeks, Karakalpaks and also Mongols (aaruul). Related products are known across a vast area, from Iranian kashk and Caucasian dried products to Middle Eastern jameed.

Unlike fresh curd cheese, kurt has almost no moisture and is salty and dense; it is sucked or chewed as a snack. Kurt is also soaked and ground in water to make a fermented milk sauce or a base for soups and dishes (such as qurutob in Tajikistan).

Frequently asked questions

What is kurt made from?

Soured milk, katyk or buttermilk: the whey is strained off, the mass is salted, shaped into balls and dried.

How long does kurt keep?

Well-dried kurt keeps for months, so it was taken on long migrations and stored for winter.

What is the difference between kurt and kashk?

They are related: both are dried, strained fermented milk products. In Iranian cooking kashk is often used as a paste or dissolved in dishes.
