🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Turkmens

People

Also known as: Turkmen

Camel milk and shubat (chal).

The Turkmen have long lived in the Karakum Desert and along its edges, combining oasis farming with herding. In the sands the main dairy animal was the camel: it copes with heat and long treks without water better than any other animal.

The Turkmen make chal from camel’s milk — a fermented milk drink close to Kazakh shubat. It is soured with a starter from the previous batch and quenches thirst well in the heat. Cow’s, sheep’s and goat’s milk is made into gatyk (katyk), süzme and butter.

Chal is still drunk in Turkmenistan, especially in rural areas and in summer. Scientists study it as one of the traditional camel milk products of Central Asia. For the Turkmen, dairy is both everyday food and part of hospitality: a guest is served tea, flatbread and fermented milk.

Frequently asked questions

What is chal?

A Turkmen fermented camel milk drink, related to shubat.

Why do the Turkmen milk camels?

In the desert the camel is the hardiest dairy animal and gives milk even in the dry season.

What is the difference between chal and shubat?

They are essentially similar drinks made from soured camel milk; the Turkmen and Kazakhs simply call them by different names.
