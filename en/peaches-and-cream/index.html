📜 «peaches and cream»

Culture and language

Also known as: blood and milk, the picture of health

Said of a healthy, rosy-cheeked person.

“Blood with milk” is said of a healthy, rosy-cheeked person with a fresh face. It is close to the English “peaches and cream”. It is most often praise for young people, a girl or a young man. The phrase is approving and informal.

The image is easy to see: skin as white as milk and a bright flush as red as blood. Together they made a picture of health and strength. In the old days this look was thought beautiful. The phrase appears in 19th-century literature. For example, the poet Nikolai Yazykov describes a princess as “blood with milk”. Dostoevsky uses it too.

A phrase with a similar meaning is “glowing with health”. The opposite is “sickly” or “pale”. People still say it today, but it sounds a little old-fashioned. It is used more often as a joke or in books. It describes a person, not a thing: “the girl is blood with milk”.
