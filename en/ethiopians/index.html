🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Ethiopians

People

Soft ayib cheese and spiced clarified butter, niter kibbeh, are the foundation of Ethiopian cuisine.

Fermented milk products play an important role in the Ethiopian diet. They are made mostly at home, by spontaneous fermentation or with a starter from the previous batch. The milk comes from cows and, in the dry lowlands, also from goats and camels; camel milk is used to make the sour drink dhanaan.

Many products are based on ergo, whole milk that has soured on its own. It is churned in calabashes into butter (kibe), and the remaining buttermilk is heated until the soft fresh cheese ayib separates. Ayib is often served with spicy dishes such as kitfo. The butter is simmered with spices — Ethiopian basil, fenugreek, korarima — to make niter kibbeh, a fragrant clarified butter that many wat stews cannot do without.

An important feature is the Orthodox fasts: on the many fasting days, believers give up milk and other animal products. Researchers note that home production methods are still poorly standardised.

Frequently asked questions

What is ayib?

A soft Ethiopian fresh cheese made by heating the buttermilk left after churning butter from sour milk.

What is niter kibbeh?

Ethiopian clarified butter simmered with spices; the base of many sauces.

Why do Ethiopians sometimes avoid dairy?

On Orthodox fasting days, believers give up milk and other animal products.
