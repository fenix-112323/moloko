🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Slovaks

People

Slovak bryndza is an EU-protected product; the dish bryndzové halušky.

Slovaks live in the mountainous country of the Western Carpathians, where sheep have grazed the mountain meadows for centuries. Shepherds known as Vlachs milked the sheep at summer camps called salaše, curdled the milk and made cheese. This gave rise to a tradition Slovaks are especially proud of.

The signature product is Slovak bryndza: a soft, salty, slightly tangy cheese made by grinding aged sheep’s cheese. Since 2008 the name “Slovenská bryndza” has been protected in the EU as a geographical indication; it must contain at least half sheep’s milk. Bryndza is the dressing for the national dish, bryndzové halušky: small potato dumplings with crispy bacon bits.

Sheep’s milk is also made into the smoked cheeses oštiepok and parenica, and the whey into a refreshing drink, žinčica. In summer, fresh bryndza is still sold at the salaše.

Frequently asked questions

What are bryndzové halušky?

The Slovak national dish: potato dumplings with sheep’s bryndza and crispy bacon bits.

What makes Slovak bryndza different?

It is a soft, ground sheep’s cheese; under EU rules it contains at least 50% sheep’s milk.

What is žinčica?

A slightly sour drink made from the sheep’s whey left over from making bryndza.
