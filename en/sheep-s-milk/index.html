🥛 Sheep's milk

Type of milk

Also known as: sheep milk, ewe's milk

The richest in protein and calcium of the common milks; almost all of it goes into cheese.

Sheep’s milk is one of the most concentrated among common dairy animals: on average it has about 5.5–6 g of protein and 6–7 g of fat per 100 g, and almost twice the solids of cow’s milk. It also has slightly more lactose than cow’s and goat’s milk. Its fat globules are on average even smaller than those of goat’s milk.

According to the FAO, sheep account for only about 1–1.5% of world milk production, but this milk goes almost entirely into cheeses and fermented products. About 61% of the world’s sheep’s milk is produced in the Mediterranean and the Middle East. It is made into Roquefort, Pecorino Romano, Manchego, feta, bryndza and many other cheeses. Thanks to its high protein and fat content, sheep’s milk yields noticeably more cheese than cow’s milk.

Sheep give little milk and are usually milked seasonally, after lambing, so sheep’s milk products tend to cost more than cow’s milk ones. Like goat’s milk, sheep’s milk contains beta-lactoglobulin and caseins similar to those in cow’s milk, so it is not considered a safe substitute in cow’s milk protein allergy.

Frequently asked questions

What cheeses are made from sheep’s milk?

Roquefort, pecorino, Manchego, feta, bryndza and many other regional cheeses. Under their naming rules, some may only be made from sheep’s milk or a mix with goat’s milk.

Is sheep’s milk healthier than cow’s milk?

It has more protein, fat, calcium and solids, so it is higher in calories. It is still unsuitable for people with cow’s milk protein allergy.
