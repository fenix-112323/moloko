🥣 Yogurt

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: yoghurt

Fermented with Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus.

Yogurt is a fermented milk product made by souring milk with two thermophilic bacteria: Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus. The milk is usually standardized, sometimes concentrated or fortified with milk powder, heated to a high temperature, cooled to about 40–45 °C, inoculated with the culture and held for several hours. Lactic acid coagulates the casein, giving a firm texture and a tangy taste.

Yogurt has been made for centuries in the Balkans, Anatolia, the Middle East and South Asia; the word itself comes from the Turkic languages. It is most often made from cow’s milk, but buffalo, goat, sheep, camel and yak milk are also used. Strained yogurt (Greek yogurt, labneh) is thicker and higher in protein.

Yogurt differs from kefir in its cultures and temperature: it contains no yeasts or kefir grains, and it is fermented warm. Yogurt is eaten on its own or with fruit and used in sauces (tzatziki, raita), marinades and drinks such as ayran and lassi.

Frequently asked questions

What bacteria are in yogurt?

Classic yogurt is fermented with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus. Some yogurts also have added probiotic strains.

What is the difference between Greek yogurt and regular yogurt?

Greek yogurt is strained to remove part of the whey. That makes it thicker, with more protein per 100 g.

What is the difference between yogurt and kefir?

Yogurt is fermented with thermophilic bacteria at 40–45 °C without yeasts; kefir is fermented with grains containing bacteria and yeasts at room temperature.
