🦘 Kangaroo

Animal

Also known as: wallaby, Macropus

A marsupial that can produce two different milks at once from neighbouring teats, for an older and a younger joey.

Kangaroos and other marsupials give birth to a tiny, almost embryonic young that finishes developing in the pouch on its mother’s milk. Their lactation is long — about 300 days in the tammar wallaby — and complex: the milk gradually changes from dilute and carbohydrate-rich to concentrated, high in fat and protein.

A remarkable feature of kangaroos and wallabies is asynchronous lactation. A female can feed a newborn in the pouch and an older young that already lives outside at the same time. The neighboring mammary glands work independently and produce different milk: thin and sweet for the baby, rich in fat and protein for the older one.

At the same time, a dormant embryo may be “waiting” in the uterus. That is why marsupials are studied as a model of how milk controls a young animal’s development.

Frequently asked questions

Is it true that kangaroos produce two different milks?

Yes: from neighboring teats a female feeds thin milk to the baby in the pouch and rich milk to the older young at the same time.

How long do kangaroos lactate?

About a year: in the tammar wallaby, roughly 300 days.

How does a joey drink milk in the pouch?

It attaches to a teat and does not let go for a long time, receiving milk in small amounts.
