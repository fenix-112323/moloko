🐂 Musk ox

Animal

Also known as: muskox, Ovibos moschatus

A large animal of the Arctic tundra.

The muskox is a large animal of the Arctic tundra of North America and Greenland; it has also been introduced to other northern areas. The female nurses her calf with rich milk that helps it survive its first cold spring.

According to published data, muskox milk is about 11–15% fat, with more protein than cow’s milk and less lactose. In fat and protein it is closer to sheep’s milk than to cow’s. Scientists studied it after training several females on a research farm to be hand-milked — ordinary milk replacers for calves and lambs do not suit muskoxen well.

Muskoxen are not milked for their milk: on northern farms, for example in Alaska, they are valued for their underwool, qiviut, one of the warmest natural fibres. It is combed out in spring during the moult without harming the animal.

Frequently asked questions

How much fat is in muskox milk?

According to studies, about 11–15% — much richer than cow’s milk.

Are muskoxen milked?

No, only for research; on farms they are valued for their qiviut underwool.

Is a muskox an ox or a sheep?

Neither: it is a separate species of bovid, closer to goats and sheep than to cattle.
