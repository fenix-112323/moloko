🍦 Ice cream maker

Job

Also known as: ice cream vendor, ice cream man

Makes or sells ice cream.

An ice cream maker is a person who makes or sells ice cream. Ice cream was once an expensive treat. Only people who had an icehouse — a building for storing ice — could afford it.

Everything changed in the 19th century. In the 1840s, small hand-cranked ice cream freezers appeared in England and America. In 1851, the Swiss-born Carlo Gatti opened a stall near Charing Cross station in London. He sold a serving of ice cream in a shell for one penny. This made ice cream affordable. Later, Gatti brought large amounts of ice to London from Norway. By the early 20th century, street ice cream sellers were a common sight in big cities such as Chicago and Sydney.

Today an ice cream maker may work in an ice cream parlour and make the ice cream there. Or they may sell ready-made ice cream from a stall, a kiosk or a van. The key thing in this job is cold. Ice cream stays firm only at low temperatures. It is stored in a freezer at −18 °C or colder and sold from freezer chests and display cases.

Frequently asked questions

What does an ice cream maker do?

They make ice cream or sell it — in a café, from a stall, a kiosk or a van.

At what temperature is ice cream stored?

In a freezer at −18 °C or colder.

When did ice cream become affordable?

In the mid-19th century: for example, in 1851 Carlo Gatti sold a serving of ice cream in London for one penny.
