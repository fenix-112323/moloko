🧀 Monterey Jack

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: Jack cheese

A semi-firm, mild white cow's milk cheese from California; its early form goes back to 18th-century Franciscan missions.

Monterey Jack is a semi-hard white cow’s milk cheese with a mild, slightly sweet flavor, originating in the city of Monterey on California’s central coast. The method is typical of young rennet cheeses: the milk is curdled, the curd is cut and stirred, partly washed, then pressed and aged for several weeks. The cheese comes out elastic, low in acidity and excellent for melting.

The earliest form of this cheese goes back to the Franciscan missions of 18th-century Alta California. In the 19th century Juana Cota de Boronda made and sold it in Monterey as “queso del país” (“local cheese”), and the businessman David Jacks set up commercial sales — by a common account, the name comes from his surname. Another version links the name to a hand press (“jack”) used for the curd.

Monterey Jack differs from Cheddar in its milder flavor and lower acidity. Variants include Pepper Jack with chili peppers, marbled Colby Jack and the aged hard Dry Jack. The cheese goes into quesadillas, nachos, sandwiches, burgers and casseroles.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Monterey Jack and Cheddar?

Monterey Jack is milder, more elastic and less acidic, and is aged only briefly. Cheddar goes through cheddaring and ripens longer, so it is firmer and sharper.

Why is it called Monterey Jack?

By a common account, after David Jacks, a California businessman who sold the cheese in the 19th century; another version points to a hand press called a “jack”.

What is Pepper Jack?

A type of Monterey Jack with hot chili peppers and herbs.
