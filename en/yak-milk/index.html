🥛 Yak milk

Type of milk

Also known as: dri milk

Rich, high-protein milk of the highlands: butter for Tibetan tea and dried cheese.

The yak is the only bovine raised on a large scale high in the mountains of Central and South Asia: on the Tibetan Plateau, in the Himalayas, in Mongolia and in the Pamirs. For local people the yak provides milk, meat, wool and transport. According to the FAO, yak milk is very similar in basic composition to buffalo milk: it is fattier than cow’s milk (usually about 6–7%, sometimes up to 9%), contains more protein, and has 5 g less water per 100 g.

Yak milk has almost twice as much β-lactoglobulin as cow’s milk and 2–6 times more lactoferrin. In China, Nepal and Mongolia there are plants producing yak milk powder.

Yak milk is made into butter, used in Tibetan butter tea with salt, as well as yogurt and dried hard cheeses such as chhurpi, which keep for months without refrigeration. The high fat content of the milk matters in the cold highlands, where food is scarce and energy needs are high. The FAO classes the yak among the “niche” dairy animals of high mountains.

Frequently asked questions

Where do people drink yak milk?

In the Asian highlands: on the Tibetan Plateau, in the Himalayas (Nepal, Bhutan, northern India), in Mongolia and in the mountains of Central Asia.

What is yak butter tea?

A traditional Tibetan drink: tea churned with salt and butter made from yak milk. It gives plenty of energy in a cold climate.
