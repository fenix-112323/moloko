🧑‍🌾 Herder

Job

Also known as: herdsman, shepherd

Grazes the herd on pasture.

A herder is a person who looks after a herd. The herder gathers the animals together, leads them out to pasture and back, and makes sure none stray or get into trouble. It is one of the oldest jobs connected with milk.

The herder chooses where the grass is best at the moment and moves the herd to a new spot in good time. They watch the animals' health, count them and protect them from predators. Herding dogs often help. They are specially trained to control the herd on a person's command. There are even competitions that judge how well a dog and its owner work together.

Different peoples have their own herders: cowboys in North America, gauchos in South America, butteri in Italy. Among nomadic peoples, herders move with the herd from pasture to pasture with the seasons. On modern farms, workers also drive cows to pasture, to the scales, to trucks or into the milking parlour. In summer many farms let cows out to graze, so a herder's work is still needed today.

Frequently asked questions

What does a herder do?

They look after a herd: lead the animals to pasture and back, choose places with good grass, and watch over their health and safety.

What are herders called in different countries?

For example, cowboys in North America, gauchos in South America, butteri in Italy.

Why does a herder need a dog?

A trained herding dog helps gather and move the herd on a person's command.
