🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Bedouin

People

Also known as: Bedu

Desert nomads: camel milk is the main food on the move.

The Bedouin are Arabic-speaking nomadic and semi-nomadic herders of the deserts of Arabia, the Syrian Desert, Sinai and North Africa. Historically their livelihood rested on camels, goats and sheep, driven from well to well.

Camel milk was the staple on the move: a camel gives milk even when pastures are scorched, and her milk can serve as both food and drink. It is drunk fresh and soured; goat’s and sheep’s milk are made into laban (sour milk), jameed (dried balls of sour curd) and clarified butter. Offering a guest milk is a long-standing sign of hospitality.

Interestingly, among the Bedouin of Jordan the share of people who digest lactose as adults is noticeably higher than among their settled neighbors, which is linked to centuries of herding. Today most Bedouin are settled, but camel milk remains part of the culture.

Frequently asked questions

What did the Bedouin eat in the desert?

The basis was dairy — camel, goat and sheep milk, fresh and soured — along with dates and bread.

What is jameed?

Dried sour curd (made from laban) that keeps for a long time; it is soaked before cooking.

Why can Bedouin digest milk well?

Many Bedouin keep the ability to digest lactose as adults, probably a result of their long herding history.
