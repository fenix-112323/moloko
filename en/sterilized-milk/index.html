🥛 Sterilized milk

Type of milk

Also known as: sterilised milk

Heated above 100 °C for a long time: keeps well but tastes "boiled".

Sterilised milk is milk heated above 100 °C long enough to destroy not only disease-causing bacteria but also most of their spores. The classic method is in-container sterilisation: the milk is filled into bottles or cans, sealed and held in an autoclave, for example at about 110–120 °C for 10–20 minutes. According to the FAO, unsweetened condensed (evaporated) milk is sterilised the same way — at about 120 °C for 13 minutes.

Long heating gives the milk a creamy tint and a distinct “boiled”, caramel taste: at high temperature lactose reacts with proteins (the Maillard reaction). Losses of heat-sensitive vitamins are greater with this treatment than with pasteurisation and UHT processing.

On the other hand, sterilised milk can be kept for a long time without refrigeration in a sealed container. Today it has largely been replaced by aseptically packed UHT milk, which preserves the taste better, but in-container sterilisation is still used for concentrated milk and some drinks.

Frequently asked questions

Can you drink sterilised milk without boiling it?

Yes: it is sterile in its sealed pack. Once opened, it must be kept in the fridge.

Why does sterilised milk taste different?

With long heating, lactose interacts with proteins, producing a creamy colour and a “baked milk” taste.
