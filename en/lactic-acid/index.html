🧪 Lactic acid

Nutrient

A product of lactose fermentation that gives a sour taste.

Lactic acid is the substance that makes fermented milk products sour. It is made by lactic acid bacteria when they feed on lactose, the milk sugar. Its chemical formula is C3H6O3. It was discovered by the Swedish chemist Carl Scheele in 1780. He found it in sour milk, hence the name: in Latin, lac means “milk”.

Lactic acid does three important things. It gives kefir, yogurt, soured milk and sour cream their sour taste. It makes the protein casein clot: milk thickens, and when it sours strongly, curds separate out. And it protects the food: harmful germs hardly grow in an acidic environment. The same trick works in sauerkraut, pickled cucumbers and sourdough bread.

Lactic acid is also used on its own. As a food additive it is called E270: it is added for flavour and to help food keep longer. It is used to make PLA, a plastic that breaks down in nature. Our body also makes lactic acid, for example in the muscles during hard work. This is a normal part of metabolism.

Frequently asked questions

Why does kefir taste sour?

Lactic acid gives it the sour taste. The starter bacteria make it from milk sugar, lactose.

What is E270?

It is lactic acid, an approved food additive. It is used for a sour taste and to help foods keep longer.
