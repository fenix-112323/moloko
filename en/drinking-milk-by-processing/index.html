🥛 Drinking milk by processing

Category

Also known as: fluid milk, drinking milk

What milk on the shelf can be: from raw to UHT, whole and skimmed.

The milk sold in shops is almost always processed. The FAO classifies it, first, by fat content — whole, standardized, semi-skimmed, low-fat and skimmed — and second, by heat treatment: pasteurized, ESL (extended shelf life), ultra-high temperature (UHT) and sterilized. Milk can also be homogenized, fortified with vitamins, lactose-free and so on.

The main purpose of heat treatment is safety. Raw milk can carry Campylobacter, E. coli, Listeria, Brucella and Salmonella; according to the CDC, pasteurization sharply reduced milk-borne illness from the early 20th century on. The stronger the heating, the longer the milk keeps, but the more its taste and some vitamins change: pasteurized milk keeps for days or weeks in the fridge, UHT and sterilized milk for months without refrigeration.

There is also gentle heating below pasteurization levels, thermization (about 57–68 °C), used as an interim step to reduce microbes before processing. Untreated raw milk is sold in a number of countries, but health authorities consider it a higher-risk product.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to drink raw milk?

Health authorities such as the CDC and FDA advise against it: raw milk can contain dangerous bacteria. It is especially risky for children under 5, older adults, pregnant women and people with weakened immunity.

Which milk has the longest shelf life?

UHT and sterilized milk: unopened, they keep for months without a fridge. Pasteurized milk keeps in the fridge for a few days to a few weeks.

What does standardized milk mean?

Milk whose fat content has been adjusted at the dairy to a set level by adding or removing some of the cream.
