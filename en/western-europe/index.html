🌍 Western Europe

Region

France, the Netherlands, Britain: the home of hundreds of cheese varieties.

Western Europe’s mild maritime climate, with plentiful rain and lush pasture all year round, is ideal for cows. No wonder one of the world’s richest cheese cultures took shape here, and Holland and France gave the world well-known dairy cattle breeds.

France is famous for hundreds of cheeses: cave-aged Roquefort made from sheep’s milk, Camembert, Brie, Comté. The Netherlands is known for Gouda and Edam, exported around the world for centuries. Britain makes Cheddar and Stilton, and tea with milk has become a national habit. Many names are protected in the EU as designations of origin: Roquefort, West Country Farmhouse Cheddar, Gouda Holland.

Cheesemaking here moved from monasteries and farms to large factories, but farmhouse raw-milk cheeses are still highly valued.

Frequently asked questions

How many types of cheese are there in France?

Hundreds; the exact number depends on how you count the varieties.

Why is there so much cheese in Western Europe?

The mild, wet climate provides pasture almost all year, and cheese is a convenient way to preserve milk.

What does a protected cheese name mean?

The name (Roquefort, for example) may only be used for cheese made in a specific area according to set rules.
