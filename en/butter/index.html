🧈 Butter

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: beurre, makkhan

80–82.5% milk fat (depending on the standard).

Butter is a solid dairy product made up of roughly 80–82% milk fat; the rest is water (about 16%) and small amounts of protein, lactose and minerals. It is made by churning cream: vigorous agitation breaks the membranes of the fat globules, the fat clumps into butter grains and the liquid (buttermilk) separates. The grains are washed, pressed and worked into a smooth mass, sometimes salted.

Butter has been known since ancient times in many herding cultures. In a number of countries traditional butter is still churned from soured whole milk. Modern industry makes sweet cream butter from unfermented cream and cultured butter from soured cream, with varying fat and salt levels.

Butter differs from ghee in containing water and milk protein: ghee is almost pure fat, so it burns less and keeps longer. It differs from margarine in being made only from milk fat. Butter is spread on bread and used in baking, sauces and frying.

Frequently asked questions

How much fat is in butter?

Usually about 80–82%; there are also lower-fat types (such as “peasant” and “sandwich” butter) and clarified butter with almost 100% fat.

How is butter made?

Cream is churned until the fat gathers into butter grains and the buttermilk separates. The grains are then washed and worked.

What is the difference between butter and ghee?

Butter contains water and milk protein, while ghee is almost pure milk fat obtained by rendering. That is why ghee withstands higher temperatures.
