😴 Sleep

Health

A folk remedy: warm milk before bed.

Warm milk before bed is an old folk remedy. Grandmothers in many countries recommend it. The usual explanation is that milk contains tryptophan. This is an amino acid — a “building block” of protein — that the body uses to make serotonin and melatonin. Melatonin is the sleep hormone.

Science is cautious here. Milk really does contain tryptophan and melatonin, but according to reviews, one glass most likely has too little of them to have a noticeable effect on sleep. There is “night milk” — milk taken from cows at night, which has more melatonin. But so far it has only been tested on animals. Small studies in people have given mixed results: for example, older people who drank a fermented milk drink for three weeks woke up less often at night.

Most likely, the ritual itself is what matters. A warm drink is calming and tells the body it is time to rest. Sleep experts believe it is far more important to go to bed at the same time every night and follow simple sleep habits. If milk causes bloating, for example with lactose intolerance, it will only get in the way of sleep.

Frequently asked questions

Does warm milk help you sleep?

Perhaps a little — mostly as a calming ritual. According to reviews, one glass has too little tryptophan and melatonin for a strong effect.

What is “night milk”?

It is milk from cows milked at night; it has more melatonin. But its effect on people has not been proven yet.
