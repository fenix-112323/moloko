🫕 Smen

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: samn, samneh

A salted, aged clarified butter of the Maghreb, usually from sheep's or goat's milk. In Morocco it is aged until pungent; old smen is valued more highly.

Smen is a salted, aged (fermented) clarified butter of the Maghreb — Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia — and also a traditional product of Yemen. It is made from butter of sheep’s, goat’s or mixed milk. The butter is brought to the boil, skimmed, strained into a ceramic jar and salted; sometimes toasted fenugreek seeds are added to the boiling butter, and thyme or other herbs as a “starter”. The smen is then aged in sealed jars; traditionally these were buried in the ground for a steady temperature, much as cheese is aged in caves.

Over time smen develops a sharp, cheesy aroma similar to that of an aged cheese; in Morocco old smen is valued more highly than young. In Jordan and Lebanon the word “samneh” refers to a simpler clarified butter that is not aged.

Smen differs from ghee in being salted and aged: ghee is fresh clarified butter, while smen “ripens”. It differs from Ethiopian niter kibbeh in getting its flavour from ageing rather than from a large set of spices. Smen is added to couscous, tagines, harira soup and pastries, often just a spoonful for aroma.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between smen and ghee?

Both are clarified butter, but smen is salted and aged until it has a sharp aroma, while ghee is used fresh.

What milk is smen made from?

Usually from butter of sheep’s, goat’s or mixed milk.

How is smen used?

A little is added to couscous, tagines, soups and pastries for a rich aroma.
