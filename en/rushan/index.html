🪭 Rushan

Dairy product 🏺 Traditional product

Also known as: milk fan, rushan cheese

The Bai people's "milk fan" from Yunnan: cow's milk is curdled with a sour solution (no rennet), stretched into thin sheets and dried on bamboo poles. Eaten fried, with sweet toppings.

Rushan (乳扇, “milk fan”) is a traditional cheese of the Bai people of Yunnan Province in China, above all from the area around Dali. It is made from cow’s milk: the milk is heated and curdled with a sour liquid, without rennet. The fresh hot curd is kneaded, stretched into thin elastic sheets, wound onto long bamboo poles and hung to dry until the sheets turn yellow and leathery. In the Bai language the cheese is called nvxseiz.

Rushan got its Chinese name from its resemblance to a folding fan. It is a popular street food: a dry sheet is warmed over charcoal until it softens, spread with rose jam, honey with rose petals, condensed milk or fruit jam, and rolled onto a stick like an ice pop. It is also deep-fried until golden and crisp.

Rushan is one of China’s few traditional cheeses. Its relative in the same province is rubing (“milk cake”), made from goat’s milk. Unlike mozzarella and sulguni, rushan is dried into thin sheets and eaten hot rather than fresh.

Frequently asked questions

What is rushan?

A dried sheet cheese of the Bai people of Yunnan: the curd is stretched into thin sheets and dried on bamboo poles.

How do you eat rushan?

It is warmed over charcoal or fried, spread with a sweet sauce (rose jam, for example) and rolled onto a stick.

Does China have traditional cheeses?

Yes, though only a few: for example, rushan of the Bai people and rubing, made from goat’s milk, in Yunnan Province.
