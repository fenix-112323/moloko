🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Odia

People

Also known as: Odia people, Oriya

The people of Odisha (India), who link rasgulla to the tradition of the Jagannath Temple in Puri.

The Odia (Oriya) are the main people of the Indian state of Odisha on the east coast. Milk sweets here are closely tied to temple tradition and festivals.

As in neighbouring Bengal, the basis of the sweets is chhena, a fresh curd cheese made from milk curdled with acid. It is cooked into rasagola — balls in sugar syrup. In Odisha it is linked to the Jagannath Temple in Puri: according to local tradition, rasagola is offered to the goddess Lakshmi in one of the rites that conclude the Ratha Yatra chariot festival. How old this custom is has not been established for certain. Another local sweet is chhena poda, baked chhena with sugar and a caramelised crust.

In 2019 India registered the geographical indication “Odisha Rasagola”, separately from the Bengali version. The sweet itself remains a shared heritage of the region. The milk for sweets here comes mainly from cows and buffaloes.

Frequently asked questions

What is chhena poda?

A sweet from Odisha: chhena is mixed with sugar and baked until it forms a caramelised crust.

When did Odisha get a geographical indication for rasagola?

In 2019, under the name “Odisha Rasagola”.

How is rasagola connected to the temple in Puri?

According to local tradition, it is offered to the goddess Lakshmi in one of the rites that conclude the Ratha Yatra chariot festival.
