📦 Distribution center

Place

Also known as: distribution centre, DC

A retail chain's large warehouse from which goods are delivered to stores.

A distribution center (DC) is a large warehouse of a retail chain. Goods arrive here from different makers and are then delivered to shops across the region. Milk from the dairy plant often reaches the shop not directly but through such a center.

In a DC, goods are split by storage conditions. There is a section for chilled foods: dairy, meat and ready meals. There is a section for frozen foods, one for fruit and vegetables, and one for “dry” goods. Milk is kept in cold rooms. Workers put together an order for each shop, so many boxes of milk, kefir and cheese, and load it into refrigerated trucks.

Some centers hold almost no stock. Goods arrive, are sorted right away and sent on. This method is called cross-docking. It helps get fresh goods to the shelf quickly. Distribution centers can be huge. For example, at the American chain Walmart, one center supplies from 50 to 125 stores. This way a retail chain can bring fresh milk to hundreds of shops every day.

Frequently asked questions

What is a distribution center?

A large warehouse of a retail chain, from which goods are delivered to shops across the region.

What is cross-docking?

A method where goods are hardly stored at all: they are sorted right away and sent on to shops.

How many stores does one distribution center serve?

It varies. For example, at Walmart one center supplies from 50 to 125 stores.
